[curious, conversational] Why would a wasp lay its egg inside a cockroach’s egg case?
[brief pause]
[explaining clearly] A small wasp called an ensign wasp puts one egg inside. When it hatches, the young wasp has a dozen or more cockroach eggs to feed on.

The young wasp uses pointed jaws with tiny teeth to cut through the shells of the individual eggs around it.

After feeding and growing, it changes into an adult inside that same case. Once that change is complete, the adult cuts a small, round hole through the case and comes out.

[warm, clear, conversational] American cockroach egg cases are one of this wasp’s targets. Those roaches leave their cases in cracks and small gaps, and they favor warm, damp places. When you inspect those areas, look for egg cases as well as live roaches.
