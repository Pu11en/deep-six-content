# Recent AI animation: evidence and fit

**The strongest fit is a recurring cartoon guide teaching one useful thing through a small story.** Paper-cutout science and stick-figure explainers are the closest educational matches; short comedy supplies a useful structure for jokes.

## Scope and verification

- Requested window: **16 September–16 October 2026**, inclusive.
- Checked 13 individual YouTube uploads through `yt-dlp`; 8 fall within the window.
- Read original YouTube descriptions and available chapters. Reviewed automatic captions for six eligible uploads: Andy Lo, Mark Ai Guy, Jacksons AI, Nate Herk, Infinity Visuals, and Granny Does AI.

- Dates below use YouTube’s `upload_date` field returned by `yt-dlp`. Indexed pages sometimes differ by one day. All retained candidates meet the requested window using the direct metadata.
- AI involvement is described by each creator; it is not an independent production audit. A reference channel shown inside a tutorial is not automatically AI-made.
- Views are a snapshot from 16 October 2026, not evidence of learning outcomes or likely Deep Six results. Headline earnings and millions-of-views claims were not verified.

### 1. Paper-cutout science: Andy Lo

**[Claude Opus 5.5 Is Insane for Educational Animations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7gmPM-Xq5Zo)** — 23 September 2026; 8:08; 64,479 views when checked.

- **Useful sections:** [build at 1:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7gmPM-Xq5Zo&t=80s); [finished narrated example around 4:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7gmPM-Xq5Zo&t=280s).
- **Actual subject:** Hawking radiation, presented with a child guide and a paper puppet-stage design. The second half covers an interactive catapult.
- **Method described:** specify a visual style, recurring character, seven timed scenes, transitions, narration, and sound. Claude generates the animation; separate Fish Audio voice clips replace the first robotic narration.
- **Why it fits:** a character can make a difficult explanation feel like a story. The same structure could follow Deep Six’s reaper through a house or garden.
- **Proposed adaptation:** “The reaper investigates the mystery of the midnight kitchen visitor.” One pest question, a comic discovery, one verified takeaway.
- **Limit:** the finished narrated version involves multiple steps, despite the opening one-prompt claim. It demonstrates a playable animation; a repeatable MP4 export workflow was not established in this review.

### 2. Simple stick-figure lessons: Mark Ai Guy

**[How to Create Stickman Animation with AI 100% FREE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H8FAaKeCguA)** — 13 October 2026; 15:52; 118,031 views when checked.

- **Useful sections:** [finance example at 1:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H8FAaKeCguA&t=80s); [finished example at 15:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H8FAaKeCguA&t=924s).
- **Actual subject:** two characters illustrating a small daily saving habit. The tutorial offers different niches, character references, durations, and aspect ratios.
- **Method described:** chatbot script and scene prompts; batch image and animation generation in Google Flow; ElevenLabs narration; CapCut assembly and captions. Eight short scenes make the example.
- **Why it fits:** simple figures and one contrast are easy to carry across topics. The lesson can change while the characters and layout recur.
- **Proposed adaptation:** two cartoon homeowners face the same pest problem, make different prevention choices, and discover why the choices matter.
- **Limit:** the narration claims 50 free daily credits, then reports a 56-credit animation request around 9:30. This does not establish a repeatable free workflow. Prompt access also requires contacting the creator; no contact was made.
