# Criteria for choosing education sources

**Use a source only if a trusted publisher says the exact thing, for Texas homes, in text we actually read, with no product or chemical instructions. Then pick the five most useful, most drawable lessons.**

Checked 19 Oct 2026. Gates and scores are our proposal built on the sources below; each rule lists what backs it. Deep Six serves New Braunfels, San Antonio, San Marcos, Seguin, Cibolo, Schertz and nearby ([services evidence](deep-six-services-evidence-2026-10-16.md)), so "Texas" here means Central Texas.

## Must-pass gates (fail any one: reject)

1. **Trusted publisher.** Publisher is on the list at the bottom. Pest-company blogs, product makers, stores, forums, social videos, and AI summaries fail. They may inspire a hook, never supply a fact.
   - Why: NPIC is run by Oregon State and EPA and gives "objective, science-based information" ([NPIC about](https://npic.orst.edu/about.html), Dec 2023). CRAAP Authority asks who the author, publisher, or sponsor is. Fact-checkers beat historians and students by leaving the page to learn about the source, not trusting logos and domain names ([Wineburg and McGrew](https://cor.inquirygroup.org/research/lateral-reading-and-the-nature-of-expertise)).
2. **Claim is in the text we read.** Open the page or section. Record a short quote. A search snippet, a summary, or model memory does not count.
   - Why: CRAAP Accuracy asks where information comes from and whether evidence supports it ([CSU Chico CRAAP, adapted](https://library.lapc.edu/evaluating-online-sources/craap-test)). DISCERN Q4 asks whether sources are clear and checkable ([DISCERN](https://www.ndph.ox.ac.uk/research/applied-health-research-unit-ahru/discern/the-discern-questionnaire)). Tool precedent: mole discards any claim whose quote is not verbatim in the page.
3. **Fits Texas homes.** The pest lives in Central Texas. Advice does not depend on another state's climate, species, or product rules. Out-of-state sources (UC IPM, other extensions) give biology only, and only after a Texas source agrees.
   - Why: NPIC says "Many states add restrictions to federally approved pesticide labels" ([NPIC label databases](https://npic.orst.edu/ingred/proddata.html)). NPIC says identify the ant first because fire-ant and carpenter-ant control differ ([NPIC ants](https://npic.orst.edu/pest/ant.html)). AgriLife timed fire-ant baiting differently for central/south versus north Texas ([AgriLife, 30 Apr 2018](https://fireant.tamu.edu/?p=1477)).
4. **Date fits the claim.** Record published, updated, or "undated." Timeless biology (what a colony is, how trails work) may be old if no newer trusted source conflicts. Product, dose, timing, law, and health claims need the current label or a source from the last 3 years (the 3 years is a proposal). Undated pages: biology only, with a second source.
   - Why: CRAAP Currency asks if the topic needs current information. DISCERN Q5 asks if dates are clear. Old advice goes stale: EPA's 2000 Dursban deal pulled most household chlorpyrifos uses ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/archive/epapages/newsroom_archive/newsreleases/880b35adc877c301852568f8005399ed.html)). Our own examples: the A&M household-ant guide is December 2005, and the 2018 A&M blog names Hot Shot, Terro, and bifenthrin. The A&M fire-ant FAQ shows no date.
5. **No product, chemical, dose, or mix in the narration.** No brand names, rates, or home recipes. The script may say "read and follow the label." It may name a type of control (bait or mound treatment) only if the source does.
   - Why: "The label is the law" and using a product against its label breaks federal law ([EPA](https://epa.gov/earthday/epa-explainshow-read-pesticide-product-label), Dec 2025). NPIC: "Do not use pesticides in any manner other than those specifically listed on the label; it is against the law" ([NPIC](https://npic.orst.edu/health/readlabel.html)). NPIC: "Do not mix up your own 'recipe'," and most misapplications reported to it come from homeowners or renters ([NPIC DIY](https://npic.orst.edu/pest/diy.html)). Illegal products lack an EPA registration number ([EPA](https://epa.gov/safepestcontrol/avoid-illegal-household-pesticide-products)).
6. **Safe words and safe claims.** Never say "safe," "non-toxic," "harmless," "guaranteed," or "permanent." Child, pet, and sting statements come only from a label, EPA, NPIC, CDC, or extension text.
   - Why: EPA treats "safe," "nonpoisonous," "harmless," "nontoxic to humans and pets" as false or misleading on pesticide labeling ([40 CFR 156.10(a)(5)(ix)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-156/subpart-C/section-156.10)). FTC: a company needs a "reasonable basis" before an ad runs ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/advertising-faqs-guide-small-business)). Texas lets its agriculture department make rules against "false, misleading, or deceptive" pest-control advertising ([Occ. Code 1951.206](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._occ._code_section_1951.206)). A&M: "no method is 100% effective all the time... no method is permanent" ([fire-ant FAQ](https://fireant.tamu.edu/manage/faq/)). **Limit:** the EPA rule is written for product labels. Whether it binds a service video is not settled here, so follow it anyway. Proposal: a Deep Six license holder reviews before anything is published.
7. **Matches IPM.** Identify first, then use low-risk steps, with chemicals later. No "spray it all" lessons. Seeing one pest is not an emergency.
   - Why: EPA IPM: set thresholds, monitor and identify, prevent, then control, with "broadcast spraying" a last resort ([EPA IPM](https://www.epa.gov/safepestcontrol/integrated-pest-management-ipm-principles), updated 22 Jul 2026). UC IPM lists six parts, starting with identification ([UC IPM](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/what-is-ipm/)). NPIC: "Has it been properly identified by a professional or an expert?" ([NPIC selecting](https://npic.orst.edu/pest/select.html)).
8. **Not a repeat.** Publication not in the [source log](../source-log.md). Alternate URLs of one publication count as one. Five cards use five different publications. A new URL for an old lesson is still a repeat.
   - Why: source-log rules and the design's failure case "two hooks sharing a source" ([design](../video-system-design.md)).
9. **No conflict.** Check one other trusted source. If it contradicts, reject or settle it and record how. If it is silent, pass and flag "single source."
   - Why: SIFT "Find better coverage" ([Caulfield, 2019](https://hapgood.us/2019/06/19/sift-the-four-moves/)). CRAAP: "Can you verify it using other sources?"

## Ranking points (only for cards that pass every gate)

Score each 0 to 2. Take the top five, from five publications, ideally five different problems. All of this is proposal.

- **Useful today:** one homeowner problem, one action done in minutes with no purchase.
- **Drawable cause and effect:** three or four visible steps (trail, gap, nest, fix). Fits the cartoon format.
- **Surprising and true:** a plain fact the source states, usable as the hook. The gate-2 quote must support it.
- **Season:** matters in Texas now or soon. Example from A&M: fire-ant baiting starts when soil is about 70 F at 1 inch.
- **Second source agrees:** 2 if a second trusted source agrees, 0 if single source.
- **Fresh:** updated in the last 3 years, or timeless biology.
- **Deep Six topic:** ants, roaches, spiders, scorpions, wasps, mosquitoes, rodents, termites ([services](deep-six-services-and-topics-2026-10-16.md)). It teaches, not pitches.

Why one idea: Mayer's coherence principle says cut extra material; segmenting says short chunks ([summary](https://lucidea.com/blog/mayers-12-principles-of-multimedia-learning), secondary). Everything else above is our judgment.

## Lesson card: what to record

Already in the [resource library](../pest-resource-library.md): source ID, link, publisher, publication/revision and checked dates, pest/species, region, one homeowner question, supported facts with page or section, practical takeaway, limits, prior-use status.

Add only these:

- **Exact quote** per fact (short, verbatim). Section reference alone cannot be audited.
- **Publisher tier** (A or B) and a note on why.
- **Date type:** published, updated, or undated; timeless biology or time-sensitive.
- **Second source:** link and result (agrees, silent, conflicts).
- **Do-not-narrate list:** brands, chemicals, doses the source names. Plus any child, pet, or sting content.
- **Hook material:** the surprising true fact, the drawable cause and effect in one line, the Texas season window.
- **Gate results and score.**

## Trusted publishers (proposal)

Tier A, fact sources:
- Texas A&M AgriLife Extension: [Insects in the City](https://citybugs.tamu.edu), [fire ant program](https://fireant.tamu.edu), [extension entomology](https://extensionentomology.tamu.edu), [AgriLife Extension](https://agrilifeextension.tamu.edu). Extension is the land-grant university's public-education arm ([USDA NIFA](https://www.nifa.usda.gov/about-nifa/what-we-do/extension)).
- [EPA safe pest control](https://www.epa.gov/safepestcontrol) and EPA label records.
- [NPIC](https://npic.orst.edu) (Oregon State plus EPA).
- [Texas Dept. of Agriculture, Structural Pest Control](https://texasagriculture.gov/RegulatoryPrograms/Pesticides/StructuralPestControlService/StructuralPestControlLicensing.aspx) for Texas rules (license needed for pest control for hire).
- [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/mosquitoes/prevention/index.html) for disease and sting health facts (it says use EPA-registered repellents and follow the label).

Tier B, biology only, needs Texas check (gate 3): [UC IPM](https://ipm.ucanr.edu) and other state extension services.

Not allowed as fact sources: pest-company blogs (including Deep Six's own), brand and store pages, forums, social videos, AI answers, Wikipedia. Use them to find leads, then trace to Tier A.

Care with Tier A pages: the A&M blog is a first-person post that names products, and the fire-ant FAQ is undated and uses brand names only as examples ("Extension does not endorse specific products"). Take behavior and identification from them, not product advice.

## Frameworks used

- **CRAAP** (currency, relevance, authority, accuracy, purpose; Meriam Library): maps to gates 1 to 4.
- **SIFT** (stop, investigate the source, find better coverage, trace claims; Caulfield, CC BY 4.0): gates 1 and 9.
- **DISCERN** (15 questions plus overall rating, for written health information on treatment choices; Charnock and others, [1999](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1756830/)): its source and date questions inform gates 2 and 4. Pest advice is not health advice, so this is an adaptation (proposal).

## Tools for the research step (short)

Catalog search, then live GitHub check on 19 Oct 2026. None run or installed.
- [lajosdeme/mole](https://github.com/lajosdeme/mole): Go, Apache-2.0, 317 stars, pushed 13 Oct 2026. README says claims whose quote is not verbatim in the source page are discarded, and a contradiction check is done. Needs paid API keys, so it needs Drew's OK. Best match for gates 2 and 9.
- [langchain-ai/local-deep-researcher](https://github.com/langchain-ai/local-deep-researcher): MIT, 9,378 stars, pushed 15 Oct 2026. Local web research and report writer. Generic.
- [assafelovic/gpt-researcher](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher): Apache-2.0, 30,177 stars, pushed 10 Oct 2026. Generic. Not from the catalog hits; found by name.
- None of these knows which publishers to trust. The allowlist and the card gates stay ours. A plain script can enforce "domain is on the list" and "quote appears in the page."

## Limits of this note

- DISCERN handbook and the JECH paper blocked (403). DISCERN facts come from the abstract and Oxford rating pages. The NIFA page timed out; its claim comes from a search summary.
- Texas statute text read through a mirror (texas.public.law), since the state site needs JavaScript. Recheck the text before relying on it.
- Mayer and Wineburg points come from secondary summaries and abstracts.
- EPA gives two dates for chlorpyrifos household cancellations (end 2000 or end 2001). Check each product.
- Not legal advice. No paid calls were made.
