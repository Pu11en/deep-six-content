3.2s Do you have moths flying around your kitchen or insects crawling inside food packages or containers?
9.8s If so, you might have pantry pests.
13.4s Pantry pests can be moths , beetles, or other insects that infest stored food products
19.2s such as flour, grains, nuts, sweets, and other pantry items.
24.5s There are several types of pantry pests
26.8s including the Indian meal moth, cigarette beetle, rice weevil,
32.3s warehouse beetle, and other beetles such as flour beetles and grain beetles.
36.9s Telltale signs of an infestation
39.9s include webbing or tiny holes in the food item or package.
43.5s The larvae and adults of some insects
46.4s produce secretions that give food a disagreeable odor or taste.
51.1s Often by the time you notice pantry pests,
54.6s they have already infested opened packages or products
57.9s that have been on the shelf for long periods of time.
61.2s If you find the main source of the pest infestation
63.7s before it spreads to other packages,
66.2s pantry pest control is relatively easy.
69.3s Simply seal up the infested container or package and throw it away.
74.7s To eliminate a pantry pest infestation,
76.8s first inspect all food packages
79.3s and destroy any that show signs of infestation.
83.8s Then remove everything from your shelves
86.1s and wash the shelves with soap and water.
88.7s Vacuum corners or crevices
91.2s to remove hidden crumbs and any pest eggs and pupae.
94.9s Prevent future pantry pest infestations
97.5s by inspecting new purchases of packages and bulk food items,
101.2s storing food in containers with tight-fitting lids,
104.6s and frequently cleaning up any spills or crumbs.
108.1s Monitor for any future pantry pests using sticky pheromone traps,
113.2s available at most garden centers and hardware stores.
116.8s For more information on how to prevent and control pantry pests, visit the UC IPM web site.
